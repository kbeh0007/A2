Speed and Strategy on Track

Qualifying lap times at Silverstone across the decades reveal the complex story of F1 regulation cycles. Times were fastest in the V10 era of the early 2000s, rose sharply when single lap qualifying formats were introduced between 2003 and 2005, then settled into a new pattern as the sport standardised its current three-session format. Each point shows the fastest Q1 time recorded at Silverstone that year.

Does where you start determine where you finish?

Each dot represents a grid position to finish position combination across all races since 2010. Bigger, darker dots mean that outcome happened more often. The dashed diagonal shows perfect grid to finish alignment.

The Most Dramatic Season — 2021

Lewis Hamilton and Max Verstappen swapped the championship lead repeatedly across 21 rounds in 2021, separated by just a handful of points for most of the year. The grey lines show the other top contenders falling away as the season wore on.

Hover any point for round by round details.

Going into the final round at Abu Dhabi, Verstappen led Hamilton by just 8 points. Hamilton led the race and was on course to be champion — until a controversial late safety car decision gave Verstappen fresh tyres and one final lap to overtake. He did. Verstappen won his first World Drivers' Championship by exactly 8 points — one of the most dramatic and controversial finishes in F1 history.

Malaysia at Full Speed — 19 Years at Sepang

From Schumacher's comeback in 1999 to Verstappen's farewell win in 2017, Sepang hosted some of F1's most iconic moments. Each row is one race, coloured by the winning constructor.

Hover any row for details.

1999 Schumacher returned from injury to help Ferrari secure a 1-2 finish. Both cars were disqualified then reinstated on appeal.
2001 A torrential tropical rainstorm mid-race turned Sepang into a survival test. Ferrari's strategic brilliance prevailed.
2009 The race was stopped after 31 laps due to extreme weather and declared under half points rules. Winner Jenson Button drove for Brawn GP — a team that appeared only this season after Honda withdrew from F1, sold for £1, and went on to win both championships.
2013 The infamous Multi 21 controversy — Vettel ignored team orders and overtook Webber, sparking a major internal Red Bull dispute.
2017 Verstappen won the final Malaysian GP, dedicating it to his father Jos. Malaysia's F1 chapter closed that afternoon.

F1 is Bigger Than Ever — But Malaysia Left Too Soon

When Malaysia left in 2017, F1 viewership was at a decade low of just 352 million. Since then, driven by Netflix's Drive to Survive and a new generation of fans, the sport has exploded in every measurable way. The shaded region on both charts marks the years Malaysia was on the calendar.

Can Malaysia Afford to Return?

F1 has never been more popular. The global fanbase exceeds 800 million, race attendance is at record highs, and revenue has more than doubled since Malaysia left the calendar. Southeast Asia has no race since Singapore dominates the region. Yet Malaysia's Sports Minister confirmed in August 2025 that hosting fees are estimated at RM300 million per year, with a typical 3 to 5 year contract costing up to RM1.5 billion total. When Malaysia last hosted, Sepang rarely filled its 130,000 seat capacity. The question is not whether Malaysia loves F1. The question is whether the sport's new golden age changes the economics enough to justify the investment, and whether private funding could finally make it happen.
